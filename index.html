How it went

Our team DC940 finished in 30th rank on Day 31. Out of 558 checks in the event’s data, we solved 558, and we cleared every single challenge on Days 1 through 31. 31 consecutive days without a miss.

The start was humbling. Day 1 put us in 270th, Day 2 in 185th we were learning the format, the lanes, and each other while the race was already running. But the curve only went one way. By Day 12 we hit 3rd. We held 3rd again on Day 14, and on Day 15 we took 1st top of the entire field, from 270th to first in two weeks.

None of that happened by luck. Our members put in real hours: reading challenges before release, cross checking each other’s work, and grinding through the binary and forensics lanes late into the night. One of our members came up with a brilliant idea built and ran an automation tool that helped us solve challenges faster submitting, verifying, and cycling through attempts at machine speed while the humans handled the thinking. The tool didn’t rank anyone; it multiplied everyone. Every improvement in its speed came from a member who stayed up finding the bottleneck.

On Day 30 we were ranked 57 and eventually ended up being ranked 6 after Sha1-Hulud challenge was solved. On Day 31 was the one that got away where we struggled with the web challenges which are Framework Roulette and Full Stack Incident. These challenges took us time to complete and after completing these last two web challenges we ended up being ranked 30. These challenges are N/A for us on the final board due to not being able to obtain the time and rank for it. Watching the clock run out on a 31 day all-clear streak stung. But it also showed us exactly where to train next season.

It was an awesome learning experience. We came for the flags and left with a better team to remember.
